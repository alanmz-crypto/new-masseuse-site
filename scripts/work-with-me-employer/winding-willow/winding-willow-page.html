Massage with Sa

I offer three kinds of massage at Winding Willow, each in 60- or 90-minute sessions:

  • Thai massage, fully clothed, on a floor futon, with rhythmic pressure and assisted stretching
  • Deep tissue & neuromuscular massage, with slow, focused work on tight, tender spots, on the table
  • Swedish massage for a gentler, relaxing session

Heated towels, hot stones or aromatherapy can be part of a session if you’d like them. On your first visit, book a “Massage Initial” appointment.

What the Research Says About Massage Alongside Other Care

Research on how massage fits with other kinds of care is limited. The studies are small, most compare one approach with another or add one kind of care to another, and most follow people for only a few weeks. The summaries below describe what the studies reported. They are general information, not medical advice or a promise of any result, and people respond differently. Each part starts with what the studies found in massage’s favor, then gives the results that were weaker, mixed or negative. I included the studies I found with disappointing results, not only the favorable ones.

Massage and Acupuncture Compared

Trials that compared Thai massage directly with another approach found that people improved with both. In 18 military personnel with back pain linked to tender spots in the muscles, five sessions of Thai massage or of acupuncture over 10 days were each followed by less pain, with the benefit beginning after the first session.[1] In a hospital trial in Bangkok, 120 adults had Thai massage or joint mobilization twice a week for four weeks, and both groups improved by similar amounts on pain and everyday-activity scores.[2]

These trials do not name a clear winner, and they have limits. In the military trial, pain ratings did not differ between the groups, but one pain-questionnaire score was lower after acupuncture, and only the acupuncture group showed a higher tolerance for pressure on tender points.[1] The Bangkok trial had no group that received neither approach, so it cannot show how much of the change was due to the sessions themselves.[2]

Neither approach came out clearly ahead, so you can choose by what feels right to you. At Winding Willow, both are offered under one roof.

Massage Added to Other Care

Three small trials added massage to other care and found more improvement than that care alone. In 80 adults with neck pain linked to tender spots in the muscles, 12 sessions of deep tissue massage on top of electrical stimulation, hot packs and ultrasound improved pain, a neck-disability score, quality of life and most neck movements more than those three alone.[3] In 60 adults with chronic low back pain, deep massage on tender points plus traction was followed by less pain and muscle hardness, and a higher tolerance for pressure, than traction alone.[4] In 41 adults with chronic low back pain who all had physiotherapy, both real and placebo massage were followed by less pain and disability, and only real massage improved quality of life.[5]

Adding other approaches to massage has been less clear. In 140 adults with low back pain, adding acupuncture to court-type Thai massage made no additional difference after one month of sessions or at a one-month follow-up.[6] In 140 older adults with low back pain, Thai massage with an herbal compress and Thai massage alone led to similar improvements over 15 weeks, so the compress added no clear benefit.[7] The trials that added massage were small and followed people for weeks, not months, and the care massage was added to was physical-therapy care, not services offered at Winding Willow.

In the larger back-pain trial, people who had Thai massage alone and people who also had acupuncture both reported clinically meaningful reductions in pain and moved more easily.[6] In the herbal-compress trial, both groups improved.[7] So adding another service is a matter of choice, not a way to get more from a massage.
